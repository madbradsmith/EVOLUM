PAGE TYPE 2 — STORY ARCHITECTURE
II · STORY ARCHITECTURE

The shape of the story.

SYNOPSIS

Hong Kong, 1985. Detective Julia Chen returns from forced administrative leave to a precinct that no longer trusts her.

Her partner — the only colleague who'd vouch for her — is dead. The case file connecting his murder to a series of waterfront killings has gone missing. The one witness who can place the killer at the scene is Marcus Ng, a man she put away seven years ago and who now refuses to speak to anyone but her.

Over seventy-two hours, Julia must decide whether to play by the rules of a system she no longer believes in, or break them in a way she can't take back.

THEME
Trust is a weapon wielded by the desperate.
CORE CONFLICT
A detective's loyalty to a system that has stopped protecting her, set against her need to solve the murder of someone who did.
STORY ENGINE
A 72-hour ticking clock — the witness will only speak to Julia, and only for as long as her badge holds.
REVERSAL
The captain who reinstated her is the architect of the cover-up. The one person she trusted to bring her back is the one she has to take down.
WORLD · TIME · SETTING
1980s Hong Kong police precinct · 72 hours · Urban, claustrophobic

PAGE TYPE 5 — ANALYSIS GRID
V · ANALYSIS GRID

Strengths versus risks.

STRENGTHS
  • Protagonist with marquee scale — built for a film actor crossing in.
  • Tight 72-hour engine; no flab in Act I or Act III.
  • Period world is specific without being archeological.
  • Theme lands without exposition. The script trusts the audience.
RISKS
  • Act II loses momentum twice — pages 52–58 and 71–76.
  • Antagonist's interior life is underdrawn; he reads as plot device.
  • One supporting role (LIA) is structurally critical but dramatically thin.
  • The reversal lands a beat too early — recover with one new scene.
STORY INSIGHTS
  • The grief is structural — the case is a vehicle for what the script is actually about.
  • Trust is the only currency in the world; every scene is a transaction.
REWRITE PRIORITIES
  • Pages 52–58: tighten or replace.
  • Add one antagonist solo scene before Act III.
  • Give LIA a goal of her own.

  • Let the hands betray what the face won't.

  • Choose when to break eye contact.

  • Being underestimated.
  • The smell of the precinct after rain.
  • "I knew your father."

  • Avoid ironic distance.
  • Don't telegraph the grief.
  • Resist explaining the silences.
READER CHEMISTRY TIPS
  • Listen at half-volume; respond at full.
  • Hold the read on the reader's wrong answer.
  • Make them earn the next line.
